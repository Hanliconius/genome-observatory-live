Step-by-step record recovery guide and reproducible commands ↗

Definitions, limitations and source coverage

First-ever: one representative of the earliest qualifying assembly per NCBI species, with no earlier dated Full GenBank genome at any assembly level found in the current/replaced summaries. Upgrade: the first qualifying assembly when an earlier scaffold/contig assembly exists for that species; it may be a new accession, not a revision. Additional: other qualifying assemblies, including same-day companions and species with earlier historical chromosome assemblies. Same-date chromosome assembly ties are resolved by accession for counting only, not as a claim of priority. A lower-level assembly on the same date is part of the first-release cohort: within-day priority is unresolved. An older version of the same accession instead establishes an upgrade.

The historical check is restricted to this research preview. The main Observatory still uses its existing chromosome/complete inventory and counters. Suppressed and Partial assemblies are excluded from historical context. Dates are the public release dates reported by NCBI; historical summaries do not establish the date every assembly first reached a particular quality level. Taxonomic reassignments and unavailable records can leave gaps.

Display precedence: explicit named programme evidence in a linked BioProject title/description or submitter → production-centre name → repository role → research origin at any project size (low-output subset shown separately) → public agency → commercial name → unresolved. Direct BioProject organisations, linked parent programmes and documented institution aliases supplement assembly submitters. No programme match means “not identified”, not “independent”. Production-centre attribution does not establish who led or funded the work.

Funding is optional and linked papers are incompletely recorded. Origin types are inferred from direct BioProject organisation and assembly-submitter names, with documented narrow aliases; source evidence and overlapping roles are retained. Repository and production-centre roles do not establish scientific leadership. Grant footprints are lower bounds within the enriched chromosome-scale inventory, not a grant’s total output. Grants are matched by agency plus identifier. Publication authors/affiliations have not been systematically mined; individual lab leadership remains unverified. See the repository’s stepwise recovery guide for rule order, examples and reproducible commands. GoaT intent tags are not used to classify the NCBI production effort.

Questions for Sybille / GoaT maintainers

  1. Can we recover original declaration dates, source-system ingestion dates, and status transitions separately?
  2. Are cancelled or withdrawn declarations recorded? Can in-progress data be shared before final assemblies?
  3. Can BioProject, umbrella initiative, scientific lead, specimen provider and sequencing facility coexist as distinct metadata?
  4. How could an i5k community list be maintained and attributed while respecting independent lab credit?

What is a “small effort”?

One-off BioProject, low lifetime species output, independent research leadership, or absence of production infrastructure? Which is most meaningful for the community?

Who did the biology?

Does NCBI's submitter name identify the collecting specialist, the sequencing provider, or the archive? Which additional record fields would resolve this?

What is missing from GoaT?

Separate assembly discoverability, project attribution and pre-sequencing intent. An assembled genome appearing in GoaT does not prove earlier intent registration.

Which taxa benefit most?

Are independent specialists especially important for poorly represented orders, genera, parasitoids, symbionts, protists, or difficult-to-sample organisms?

Can we measure advance notice?

Ask the GoaT team whether historical first-seen dates and status transitions can be obtained, including DToL ingestion dates versus biological events.

What would persuade a lab?

Useful discovery by collaborators, less duplicated sampling, earlier sharing of expertise, better credit, and a genuinely simple registration route.
